\section*{Limitations}

\paragraph{The definition of an injection is a choice.}
Our pairs label an instruction by whether following it serves the user's request. Under the strict view that every
instruction in data is untrusted \citep{chen2024struq}, both members of every pair are injections, and a detector that
flags requested instructions is behaving correctly. The pair-based hypotheses (H2, H3 on pairs, H4) are meaningful
only under our definition; the artifact analysis, the \vthree{} gates and transfer to agent benchmarks are not
affected by it. We exclude instructions that are unsafe to follow even when requested, but deployments that never act
on data-borne instructions lose nothing by flagging aligned ones.

\paragraph{Pairs are LLM-drafted.}
Request phrasing may carry generator artifacts, and a model trained on pairs from one generator could learn that
generator rather than intent. We separate evaluation and training generators and templates, keep only pairs on which
two human annotators agree, remove request-only and content-only shortcuts by construction, and report lexical
baselines and a subtle subset. These measures cannot rule out every generator artifact.

\paragraph{Small and synthetic evaluation data.}
BIPIA-derived test splits are small (\vtwo{} test has \val{VTwoTaskEmailNdocs} EmailQA documents), so per-task
intervals are wide and per-task results are descriptive only. We use three of BIPIA's five tasks. Table clean-B
spans in \vthree{} are templated, and inserting text into tables and newline-free emails is unnatural. The agent
benchmarks are simulations with static attacks; the $\tau$-bench injection point is chosen by the set builder. Our
TPR values are therefore upper bounds against adaptive attackers, and we make no adaptive-attack claim.

\paragraph{Detectors whose training data cannot be audited.}
PIGuard was trained on most BIPIA and InjecAgent attack strings \citep{liu2026passing}, and some public detectors do
not disclose their training data. We flag these detectors in every table and never call them zero-shot.

\paragraph{No frontier-LLM comparator.}
The request-aware comparators are open 7--8B LLM judges, a small classifier with a goal segment, and our own
ablation. The strongest request-aware detectors in practice are large API models prompted with the request; to keep
the project at no monetary cost we do not evaluate one, and we make no claim of superiority over them.

\paragraph{Calibration.}
Calibrated thresholds for agent sets come from a synthetic pool of clean tool outputs, whose distribution may differ
from the benchmarks' clean outputs, so the realized false-positive rate can miss its target. We report the realized
rate next to every calibrated TPR.
